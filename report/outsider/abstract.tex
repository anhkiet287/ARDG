\chapter*{Abstract}

This thesis studies cross-attack adversarial robustness in a bounded
CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 setting. The main comparison evaluates four methods under a
shared training and evaluation protocol: PGD-AT, DDN-AT, Multi-AT, and
AttackDRO++. Multi-AT trains uniformly on PGD-$\ell_\infty$ and DDN-$\ell_2$
source attacks, while PGD-AT and DDN-AT serve as single-source specialists.

\noindent AttackDRO++ is proposed as a latent-group refinement of Multi-AT. It
clusters adversarial examples, augments the grouping signal with gradient
fingerprints, and uses a uniform anchor so that the adaptive group-weighted
objective remains tied to the underlying multi-attack training signal. The goal
is not to replace the multi-attack baseline, but to test whether finer latent
groups can improve cross-attack balance under the fixed protocol.

\noindent Across the paired-seed experiments, AttackDRO++ shows a small positive
Mean(8) trend over Multi-AT and lower observed seed-to-seed variation on the
average-case metrics. The hardest-case metric, Worst(8), and the separate
AutoAttack-$\ell_\infty$ checks remain broadly comparable to Multi-AT.
AutoAttack is reported separately from Mean(8), so it is treated as a stress
test rather than part of the main aggregate ranking. These results support a
conservative conclusion: in the evaluated CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 setting,
AttackDRO++ provides a modest average-case refinement of Multi-AT without
establishing broader robustness claims.
