\chapter{Background}
\label{chap:background}
This chapter introduces the background needed to understand the proposed cross-attack
adversarial training framework. It first reviews the supervised classification setting,
adversarial examples, threat models, and adversarial training. These concepts establish
why robustness is usually defined relative to a specific perturbation set and attack
algorithm. The chapter then explains why robustness obtained from a single attack may
not transfer uniformly to other attacks, motivating the multi-attack setting used in this
report. This transition is important because the proposed method does not treat attacks
only as evaluation tools, but also as sources of training-domain variation that can expose
different weaknesses of the model. Finally, it introduces distributionally robust
optimization, Group DRO, attacks-as-domains, latent group discovery, and the statistical
tools used to evaluate multi-seed robustness results. Together, these foundations prepare
the reader for the AttackDRO++ methodology developed in Chapter~\ref{chap:methodology}.
